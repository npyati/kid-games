# World Creator

A 3D island builder. Pick one of ten islands or four big maps, then fly a camera around it and fill it with trees, buildings, people, creatures and mythical monsters, then set off tornadoes, tsunamis, bombs and earthquakes. People and animals walk around on their own, birds and dragons fly, and dolphins, sharks and fish swim in the sea.

**Play it:** https://npyati.github.io/kid-games/world-creator/

## Controls

- **Xbox controller:** left stick moves the camera, right stick turns and tilts it, LT zooms out and RT zooms in, click the left stick to move faster. **A** builds where the circle is (hold A and move to keep building, which is handy for forests), **X** turns the eraser on and off, **B** (or View) undoes, **LB / RB** change group, **d-pad left / right** pick a thing, **d-pad up / down** change its size, **Y** turns it, **Start** opens the menu. Menus work with the d-pad or stick, A to choose and B to go back.
- **PlayStation controller (DualShock 4, DualSense):** works the same way, with ✕ for A, ◯ for B, ▢ for X, △ for Y, L1 / R1 for LB / RB, L2 / R2 for LT / RT, Create for View, Options for Start and R3 for clicking the right stick. The game spots a PlayStation controller by its name and shows those button names in its hints. Browsers that give it a different button order (Firefox) are put back into the standard order, including a d-pad that comes in as one "hat" axis.
- **Keyboard and mouse:** WASD or the arrow keys move, hold the right (or middle) mouse button and drag to turn, mouse wheel (or Page Up / Page Down) zooms, Q / E turn, Shift moves faster. **Click** builds where the mouse points (hold and drag to keep building). 1–5 or Tab change group, [ and ] pick a thing, - and + change size, R turns it, X is the eraser, Z or Backspace undoes, Esc opens the menu.
- **Touch:** drag with one finger to move, pinch to zoom, twist two fingers to turn, tap to build.
- Browsers only allow sound after a click or key press, so click once if the game is silent.

**🎲 Random** picks any of them. Each new island gets its own random seed, so two Tropical Paradises are different shapes. New islands start with trees and plants that fit them (up to 900: pines and oaks in the Big Forest, snowy pines on the Snowy Mountains, cactuses in the desert, lollipop trees in Candy Land, and on the big maps each land type grows its own), and islands with hills get a few **caves** (up to 6 on Pangea). Animals that fit the island come out of the caves now and then, up to 3 near each cave: bears and foxes in the forest, polar bears in the snow, goblins and trolls on the volcano, crocodiles and frogs in the swamp and so on. Caves are also in Buildings. Island Set, Pangea and Sky Islands start with their land types already coloured in.

**Size:** islands made now are much bigger than the first ones: about 2½ times as wide (around 6 times the land; the Big Forest is about 2.6 km² of land), on a 1100×1100 grid over 3.2 km (3.6 km for the big maps). Islands keep the size they were made at (`v` in the list: 1 = the first ones, 2 = 1¾ times as wide, 3 = now), so nothing on an old island moves.

## Things to build

- **Nature:** land types you paint on (hold the button to keep painting): **forest, taiga, jungle, savanna, desert, swamp, mountain, path, cobblestone path, corrupted land** and **lava**. Each colours the ground and grows its own plants (each an ordinary thing you can erase). Mountains also raise the land, swamps dig little ponds, paths flatten the ground and clear plants away, and corrupted land is dark purple with dark crystals: skeletons, demons and zombies climb out of it (up to 4 near each patch). Lava glows and flickers, burns up whatever is on it when you paint it, and nothing walks or gets put onto it. Plus land tools: **raise land, lower land, flatten, shallow water** and **deep water** (the sea is at 0 m, so anything dug below that fills with water).
- **Treasure** (this group replaced Plants; trees and plants now only grow from the land types you paint, and ones already on old islands stay): small, medium, big and huge **gold bags** (50, 150, 400 and 1,000 gold), **treasure chest** (300), **big treasure chest** full of gold and gems (1,200), **pile of gold** (600), **golden crown** on a cushion (800), giant **ruby**, **emerald**, **sapphire** (1,500 each) and **diamond** (2,500) that glow, and **gold dragon** and **gold hydra statues** (3,000 each). Bigger sizes are worth more (Big 1¾×, Giant 3×). Treasure within 90 m of a town goes into that town's gold (checked every 1½ seconds, with a toast and a line in its history): gold bags are spent and disappear, everything else stays in the town as part of its treasury. Inspect shows what treasure is worth and whose treasury it's in.
- **Buildings:** little hut, cottage, big house, barn, castle (walls, four towers with flags, a gate and a keep), watch tower, windmill (the blades turn), lighthouse (the lamp glows), tent, well, fence, campfire (flickers), gold statue, market stall, sailboat (goes on deep water and bobs).
- **People:** **human, elf, dwarf, orc**. They build towns (see Towns). Plus the **🪄 Battle wand** (see Towns). The older people (villager, farmer, knight, king, queen, wizard, kid, pirate, builder) still work on islands that have them.
- **⭐ Legendary:** **Cerberus** (❤️ 10,000 at Medium: a huge black three-headed dog with glowing red eyes, fangs, spiked collars, a shaggy mane, spines down its back and a furry dog's tail that curves up and swishes (faster when it's fighting); about 2.5 m tall at Medium (a bit taller than a horse), much smaller than the dragons. Its three heads look about on their own, and its bites work like the hydra's: one head rears back and lunges while the other two snarl, a different head each time), four new dragons (Wildwood's dragon in new colours, with the same flying, fighting and fire-at-the-sky habits as the red one, each with its own breath): the **ice dragon** (icy blue: a frost blast that hurts, **freezes** creatures solid and frosted white for 3 seconds, and puts out fires), the **lightning dragon** (dark purple and gold: jagged, branching **lightning bolts** that hit hard, jump to up to two things nearby, crack with thunder and can set plants on fire), the **water dragon** (sea blue and teal: a **water jet** that hurts, pushes things back and puts out fires), and the **golden dragon** (❤️ 25,000: gleaming gold, breathing golden fire, and about a third bigger than the other dragons). The other new dragons have ❤️ 23,400 like the red one, and each kind can have up to 3 on an island. And the **Roc** (❤️ 9,000): a giant golden-brown eagle with a white head and crest, a hooked golden beak, fierce eyes, a fanned tail, great talons and huge wings with a golden band, soaring high.
- **🛸 Conspiracy Theories:** **Loch Ness Monster** (❤️ 20,000 at Medium; deep water only; up to 3 per island. She **lurks deep under the water**, where nothing can attack her and only ripples now and then show where she is, until prey comes near: then she rises up with a big splash and attacks, and about 6 seconds after there's nothing left to hunt she sinks out of sight again. With Fighting off she comes up for a look every half a minute or so. a humped body with flippers, a very long bendy neck that sways, rears back and strikes, a detailed head (yellow eyes under heavy lids, brow ridges, a row of little bumps over the head, spots, raised nostrils, a pale chin) with a real jaw that opens, pink gums, two rows of teeth and a tongue, and a very long swishing tail; always named Nessie), **Kraken** (❤️ 19,500; deep water only; up to 3 per island; about two-thirds the size it first was; it can come into water just 1 m deep, and its grabbing tentacle stretches up to about 25 m: a huge head with glowing yellow eyes, slit pupils and angry brows, and eight long bendy tentacles that wave and curl. It can hold **up to 8 things at once**, one per tentacle: when it catches something (anything near the shore or in the water that isn't flying or huge), the free tentacle pointing nearest to it reaches out, wraps around it and lifts it out of the water beside the head, and after a 3-second hold squeezes it every second. It keeps hunting with its free tentacles until all eight are full. Nothing else can attack what it's holding), **Bigfoot** (❤️ 280: big and shaggy, with an ape-like face (a heavy brow ridge, deep-set eyes with a glint, a flat nose with nostrils, lips, a leathery face and furry cheeks and chin), ears, a pointed crest of fur on its head, a hump of muscle behind its neck, a broad chest, layered fur in three browns all over with shaggy fringes hanging from its arms, big hands with fingers and a thumb, and huge bare feet with five toes), **Alien** (green, with big dark eyes, antennae and a silver suit; ❤️ 100; its original sci-fi blaster shoots glowing green bolts from up to about 15 m, with a pew sound) and **Chupacabra** (❤️ 600: a hunched, lean wolf-beast a bit bigger than a werewolf: a long wolf snout with a wet nose and wrinkles, an open mouth with a tongue, fangs and teeth, pink inner ears, glowing red eyes under heavy brows, ribs showing, tufts of mangy fur, a pale belly and a dark stripe down its back, two rows of spines down its back and tail with bone-coloured tips, muscly arms with elbows, furry forearms and long curved claws, and wolf legs that bend backwards at the hock, with toes, claws and a dewclaw; it hunts like a lion). Nessie, the Kraken, Bigfoot and aliens fight like monsters. Aliens get names like Glorp-46.
- **Special effects:** **🌧️ Rain**: a dark cloud that rains for 20 seconds over a circle (18 m across at Medium, bigger with size) and puts out every fire under it with a hiss of steam. Fires can't start in the rain, and a dragon's breath fizzles in it.
- **Fire:** in a fight, a **dragon** breathes fire every 7–14 seconds instead of biting (a cone of flame about 22 m long at Medium). It burns anything in it (not other dragons, phoenixes or demons) and sets fires: on the ground where it lands, and on trees, plants and buildings it hits. Fires burn trees, plants and buildings down slowly, hurt people and animals standing in them (they run), can spread to plants nearby, and burn out after 35–60 seconds. With Fighting off, dragons just breathe fire at the sky now and then, and fires only make animals run. Fires aren't saved.
- **Mythical:** **dragon** (Wildwood's, full size: walks on its folded wings, flies off to circle now and then, bendy neck and tail, a jaw with teeth that rears back and lunges; it attacks on sight and comes down out of the sky to do it), **sea serpent** (Wildwood's Leviathan at about 0.9x: horns, two pairs of wings, a body that follows its head, rears up to bite), unicorn, phoenix (glows), goblin, skeleton, zombie, demon (wings, glowing eyes), troll (smaller than before), minotaur, hydra (built like the dragon but smooth and high-detail: three long bendy necks, each head looking about on its own with curling horns, glowing slit eyes, teeth and a jaw that opens; the biting head rears back and lunges while the others hiss; armoured plates and spikes down its back, clawed legs, and a swishing tail with a spiked club), ghost (see-through), **griffin** (big: lion body, eagle head and wings; hunts like a lion), **pegasus** (a white winged horse that flies high, legs tucked up), **mermaid** (swims with her head out of the water, her tail beating, and leaps now and then), **yeti** (big, shaggy and white with a blue face and little icy horns; a monster), **fairy** (tiny, glowing, with a wand and fluttering wings; flies low).
- **Creatures:** dog, cat, sheep, cow, horse, deer, bear, polar bear, fox, goat, pig, camel, bunny, squirrel, lizard, frog, crocodile (Wildwood's, with its jaw and swishing tail), crab, chicken, penguin, dinosaur, bluebird, parrot, seagull, dolphin, shark, plus lion, tiger, wolf, elephant, giraffe, zebra, panda, raccoon, hippo, turtle, llama, kangaroo, monkey, flamingo, duck, owl, eagle, butterfly, sea turtle and tropical fish. Lions, tigers and wolves are hungry hunters like bears (but they leave animals much bigger than them, like elephants, alone). Hippos, turtles and ducks can walk into shallow water.
- **Disasters** (this group replaced Weapons; weapons already on old islands stay there):
  - **Tornado:** a spinning grey funnel that wanders for about 20 seconds (longer when bigger). It picks up people, animals and plants, spins them up and throws them 25–50 m (plants break apart; anyone thrown into the sea is gone), and tears buildings down. Dragons, hydras and other huge things just get hurt.
  - **Tsunami:** finds the nearest open sea and sends a wave 90 m wide (bigger with size) rolling in at 30 m/s, past where you tapped. It knocks buildings and plants down and carries people and animals along, then puts them down (if that's in the sea, they're gone).
  - **Bomb:** falls from the sky and blows up: a crater 10 m across (at Medium), wrecks what's close, and makes everything nearby run.
  - **Sprinkle bomb:** 14 little bombs scattered over about 24 m.
  - **Earthquake:** a crack opens across the ground and the land buckles, the screen (and controller) shakes for 6 seconds, buildings fall down and people run.
  - The size buttons make them all bigger. **Undo** puts back everything a disaster wrecked and the land it changed.

Four sizes: Small (½), Medium, Big (1¾) and Giant (3×). People, creatures and mythical creatures are 0.7 times what their size says (so the islands are more to scale), except dragons, hydras and sea serpents, which are about twice as big, and trolls (0.62). Islands saved before people got smaller shrink their people and animals to match when they load. New things face the camera; **Turn** adds 45° each press. Land things only go on land, and the sailboat and sea creatures only go in deep water with room to swim: a sea creature can't go on top of another one. The circle turns red if it can't go there. Birds and dragons can go anywhere. Frogs and crocodiles can walk into shallow water. There's **no limit** on what you can place, except **3** dragons, **3** hydras, **3** sea serpents, **3** Loch Ness Monsters and **3** Krakens per island (towns, caves and animal spawns stop adding things by themselves past 6,000). Very full islands can get slow, and if an island gets too big for the browser to save, a message says so.

## Inspect

Press **🔍 Inspect** (I on the keyboard; on the controller X goes eraser, then inspect, then back to building). While inspecting, nothing gets built: the pointer turns into a magnifying glass, a banner says you're inspecting, the building bar fades, and whatever is under the pointer flashes. Click something: it picks what you see under the pointer on the screen (people and animals first), not the ground behind it. Clicking a town hall shows the whole town. A panel on the right shows it and keeps updating; ✕, B or Esc closes it.

- **People, animals and monsters:** a name (people get names in their own people's style; monsters get names like Grimjaw; mermaids and fairies get elf names; animals get pet names; kings and queens keep their royal name), an age (everyone gets older: one year every 2 minutes; dragons and hydras are hundreds of years old), a personality and something they like (people only; animals and monsters don't have those), a job (townsfolk; soldiers and rulers too), their home town, health (how much they have out of how much they can have, like ❤️ 900 / 2,340: a deer has 35, a knight 100, a troll about 280, and a hydra, sea serpent or dragon over 20,000 (they also hit three times as hard as before); bigger sizes have more), what they're doing right now, and their story: where they were born, being crowned, joining the army, battles marched in, enemies beaten (or meals caught, or victims bitten), being turned into a zombie, getting picked up by a tornado, surviving a tsunami, running from ghosts.
- **Towns:** their people, population, soldiers, buildings, farms (fields and harvests), **money** in gold (towns earn gold from their people, houses and market stalls or forges, and lose it while at war), rulers, whether they're **at war** (and with whom), keeping the peace, at peace with friends, or **neutral**, when they were founded, and a **history** of the last 12 things that happened (founded, town hall finished, crowned, wars declared, peace made, armies sent, buildings knocked down, disasters, rulers defeated).
- **Buildings and plants:** what they are and which town they belong to.

## The brush

The **Brush** panel on the left picks how many things go down at once: **1, 2, 3, 6 or 10**, spread around a circle (bigger things get a bigger circle). For Nature it sets how wide you paint (4 to 40 m). The eraser uses it too (one thing, or everything in the circle). Controller: d-pad up / down. Keyboard: C (Shift+C for smaller). Everything done in one press or hold undoes together; land changes keep the last 6 for undo.

## Fighting

- **Monsters** (demon, minotaur, yeti, dragon, hydra, sea serpent) and the **undead** (skeleton, zombie) attack people and animals on sight. **Zombies** turn anything they bite into another zombie. **Anything hostile (monsters, the undead and hungry animals) also goes after any creature smaller than itself**, other monsters too (but not its own kind), so a dragon will hunt goblins and minotaurs.
- **Orcs** fight every other kind of people and the monsters, but are friends with **goblins and trolls**. Goblins and trolls fight humans, elves and dwarves.
- **Humans, elves and dwarves** get along unless their towns are at war, and fight back against anyone who attacks. **Elves are archers**: they shoot arrows from up to 14 m away.
- **Hungry animals** (lion, tiger, wolf, griffin, bear, polar bear, fox, crocodile, shark, dinosaur) hunt people and other animals; a smaller animal is caught in one bite. People and monsters fight back. Other animals never fight; they run away when hit. Anything near a **ghost** runs away screaming ("AAAH!").
- Health bars show over anything that's hurt; beaten things vanish in a puff of smoke. Bigger things are stronger.
- **Fighting: On / Off** (title and menu) stops all fighting, monster spawning and wars.

Every time you reach a level's goal again you complete it again. A 🏆 message says when.

Each shows its name and price at the top, what it is in the middle, and little pictures of the creatures inside at the bottom (real 3D pictures the game draws of each creature). Everything else is listed underneath, creatures with their own little picture.

## Towns

**Buildings never go inside each other.** Each building has a round footprint (a castle has a bigger one), and a town only puts up a new building where it fits clear of the others. If a town building ends up overlapping another (say the town hall grew into a castle around it), it moves to a free spot in the town within a couple of seconds. Buildings you place can't go on top of another building either (the circle turns red, and a message says there's no room); fences, campfires and boats don't count, so fences still join up.

Town buildings sit on **level ground**: when a town builds on a hill (or the land under a building changes later), the ground under the building is smoothed flat, like a little terrace, so nothing sinks into the hill. These changes are saved with the island's other land changes.

- **Towns: People build** (the default; change it on the title screen or in the menu): putting down humans, elves, dwarves or orcs just puts down people (the brush puts down 1 to 10). People who aren't in a town drift toward others of their kind. Once **10** of a kind are within 35 m of each other they start a town: the centre building rises out of the ground over **2 minutes** while they walk over and hammer at it (its label shows 🔨 and how far along it is). Then the town grows on its own, slower than Quick towns: each new house rises over 20 seconds, and new people only come when houses are finished. People put down within 70 m of a town of their own kind move in.
- **Towns: Quick**: putting down a human, elf, dwarf or orc founds a town at once (or joins one of its own people's towns within 70 m): a centre building (human town hall with a clock tower, elf tree hall with glowing lanterns, dwarf mountain hall with fires, orc war camp with banners) and a few townsfolk.
- Every few seconds the town builds a house in its own style (human cottages, houses, barns, wells and market stalls; elf houses on stilts and towers; dwarf stone houses and forges; orc huts and watch towers), up to 26, and gets more people (2 per house, up to 40). Its name and population float above it, like **Brook Creek 17**, with ⚔️ when it's at war and 🕊️ when it's keeping the peace.
- **Orc towns** are at war with every town that isn't orc. Now and then a **human town** declares war on another human, elf or dwarf town for a minute or so. Towns at war send half their people marching to the enemy every 20 or so seconds; with nobody left to fight, they knock the enemy's buildings down. A town with no buildings and nobody left is gone.
- **Kids:** a town always has kids: once it has 4 people, babies keep being born (even when the town is full) whenever fewer than about a third of its people are kids. People who reach 85 move away to a quiet cottage by the sea (it goes in the town's story).
- **Growing up:** the first people in a town are grown-ups (18 to 70). After that, half of the new people are **born in the town** as babies and half **move in** as grown-ups. Everyone gets a year older every 2 minutes; kids are drawn smaller and grow as they get older, toddle about or play, and don't fight. **Only people 18 or older can have a job**: farmer, builder, soldier, king or queen, or marching to war. A town trains about 4 in 10 of its grown-ups as soldiers. Inspect shows a kid's age and how long until they can have a job.
- **Farms:** towns build **crop fields** (humans most of all, plus windmills), and **barns** that come with 3 cows, sheep, pigs or chickens. Each field gets a farmer who walks out, hoes the ground and tends it; the crops grow over about a minute (a field with no farmer grows slowly by itself), turn golden, and the farmer brings in the harvest for 40 gold. Farmers aren't picked to be soldiers or rulers. Crop fields are also in Buildings.
- **Kings and queens:** once a town has finished its centre and one more building and has 4 people, it crowns a **king**, a **queen** or both, each with a name (like King Aldric or Queen Elowen, in their people's style) shown over them, and a crown that sits on top of the head, plus a long royal robe (red for kings, purple for queens) with gold trim and a white fur collar. The town hall (or tree hall, dwarf hall or war camp) **becomes a castle**, and the rulers **live inside it, out of sight**: they come out for a short walk around the castle every minute or two, and stay out whenever their town is at war or a fight comes to them. While they're inside, their 👑 name shows over the castle (with a 🏰), so you can always see who rules. A **golden crown** treasure claimed by the town makes their crowns **grander** (taller, with pearls, jewels all round, a velvet cap and a little cross on top). They're 2½ times as tough as their people. If they're beaten, everyone hears, and the town crowns someone new 40 seconds later.
- **Armies:** a town with a ruler trains a soldier every 8–12 seconds until nearly half the town are soldiers (in armour: a helmet with a plume, a breastplate, shoulder guards, a belt and hip plates, with a sword and shield; half again as tough and hitting half again as hard). Soldiers **stand guard** at posts in a ring around the town, looking outward and pacing a couple of steps now and then. **Townsfolk don't fight**: only soldiers and rulers do. Townsfolk run from monsters and hungry animals that come close, and soldiers from other towns leave them alone. Nobody walks through buildings (except crop fields, where the farmers work); people steer around them. When the town is at war, the ruler calls out ("King Aldric: Attack Mill Ford!") and sends the army; towns without soldiers send half their people instead.
- **🪄 Battle wand** (in People): tap it on one town, then on another, and they go to war for 3 minutes (even if one was keeping the peace, and even if they were friends). Tap the same town twice to cancel.
- **🕊️ Peace wand** (in People): the opposite. Tap two towns and they become friends for good: they stop fighting, their armies go home, and they never go to war with each other again (even orcs), until the battle wand says otherwise. The Towns panel shows who is friends with whom.
- **A town falls** when its last person is gone: its town hall explodes, the screen shakes and you get "💥 Brook Creek has fallen!". Its houses stay, empty.
- The **🏰 Towns** panel (button at the top, T on the keyboard, View on the controller) lists every town with its population and who it's at war with, and has **🕊️ Make peace** (it never fights, and nobody marches on it), **⚔️ War with everyone**, **Normal** and **📍 Go there**.

## Music

A little band made in Web Audio (no recordings), in a room with reverb: a harp (plucked-string sound) plays the chords up and down, soft strings hold them, a plucked bass plays under them, and a breathy flute plays the tune on the second and third time through. 16 bars in C major at about 80 beats a minute.

## How things move

- People and land animals wander within about 20–30 m of where you put them (further for big ones), stopping now and then, and never walk into the sea or up cliffs. Legs swing as they walk; bunnies, squirrels and frogs hop.
- Birds and dragons circle where you put them (dragons fly higher and wider) and flap their wings.
- Dolphins swim and leap out of the water now and then; sharks, sea turtles and fish swim. Their tails really swim: dolphins beat their tails up and down, sharks and fish swish theirs side to side, and sea turtles flap their front flippers. The shark's tall fin shows above the water.
- **Making water washes things away:** Shallow water, Deep water and Lower land remove the trees, plants, buildings and rocks the water now covers (with a splash). Creatures, boats and treasure stay (sunken treasure!). Undo brings it all back.
- **Land creatures swim:** a person or land animal that ends up in the water (dug out from under it, dropped by a tornado or washed off by a wave) swims slowly to the nearest land, then walks again. People do a front crawl (lying forward, arms windmilling over the water, rolling with each stroke, legs kicking); animals do a doggy paddle (nose up, rocking, all four legs paddling). Swimmers leave ripples and little splashes, wade the last bit in shallow water, and don't fight while swimming. If their home spot is now under water, they live where they climbed out.
- The windmill's blades turn, campfires flicker, the lighthouse lamp, wizard's staff and treasure glow, and the sailboat bobs.
- Things you place pop in with a little bounce.

## How it's drawn (sharper than Wildwood)

- Plain WebGL 2 in one file. The noise, shape helpers, sky, water, matrix and controller code come from Wildwood.
- Up to 2× the screen's pixels (Wildwood stops at 1.5×), with antialiasing.
- **Real shadows** from a 4096×4096 shadow map with soft edges. The shadow area follows the camera: tight and sharp when you're close, wide when you're far out.
- The island is one 900×900 grid (about 2.4–2.8 m per square; old islands 560×560 at 2.2 m; Wildwood uses 2 m squares only near you). Sky Islands leave out the triangles between an island and the sea floor and add a rocky underside and rim instead, with smooth lighting. Its height comes from `islandHeight`; everything that needs the ground height uses `groundAt`, which reads the same triangles that are drawn.
- Shapes are smooth (balls with 320 faces and smooth normals, 16-sided tubes) instead of Wildwood's chunky low-poly look.
- The sea is drawn to the horizon over a sea floor that goes on forever, and each island has its own sky and water colours.

## Saved data

`localStorage` keys (all start with `worldcreator`):

- `worldcreator.v1`: settings: music, sounds, fighting, and how towns are made (`build`: true = People build).
- `worldcreator.islands`: the list of islands: `{ id, type, seed, name, count, last, v }` (`v` is the island size: 3 = now, 2 = the middle size, missing = the first, smallest ones). Broken entries are dropped on load.
- `worldcreator.island.<id>`: one island: its type and seed, the camera, its towns (`{ id, race, name, x, z, mode, built, build, friends }`, where `build` is how far the town centre is built, 0 to 1), `sz: 2` once its people and animals are the new smaller size, the land changes (each brush stroke, replayed on load), and the things on it as `[type, x, z, turn, size, town or 0, extra]` (extra: `{ k: "king" | "queen", n: name }` for rulers, `{ s: 1 }` for soldiers, plus a living thing's story: `nm` name, `ag` age, `tr` personality, `lk` likes, `bi` born in, `mv` the town they moved to, `gn` boy or girl, `zb` what it was before it became a zombie, and counts `ki`, `ba`, `to`, `wa`, `sc`, and `cl`: the town whose treasury a treasure is in, so it's only counted once). Towns also keep `gold`, `age` (seconds) and `log` (their history) (where you put them, not where they've wandered to). Unknown types, bad numbers and positions far off the map are dropped on load, and sizes are kept between ¼ and 4.

**My islands** lists them with Play and Delete (Delete asks twice). The island saves a few seconds after each change, when you open the menu, and when you leave the page.

## Testing notes

Tested in headless Chrome (SwiftShader): every island builds (in about 0.1 s) with no errors; one of every thing was placed and checked in screenshots; 60 seconds of life with walkers, swimmers and fliers had no walkers in the sea, no swimmers on land and no fliers too low; saving and loading kept every thing; a pretend controller built, switched groups, erased and undid.

## Drawing speed

Copies of the same shape (all the oak trees, all the deer legs) are drawn together in one go (instanced drawing), and things far from the camera, and all shadows, use a simpler low-detail copy of their shape. Things too small to see are skipped, and flowers and little plants cast no shadow. On a busy island (about 1,100 things and three towns) this took a frame from about 32 ms to about 15 ms on a laptop graphics chip.
